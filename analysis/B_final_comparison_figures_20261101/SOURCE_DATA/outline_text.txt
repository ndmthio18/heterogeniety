Title
Solute redistribution across a vertical textural interface during saline water evaporation
or Evaporation-driven solute redistribution across a vertical textural interface: MRI observations and numerical analysis
Target journal: Water Resources Research
Scope. Combine time-resolved MRI observations with DuMuˣ simulations to explain dissolved-salt redistribution in a continuously supplied, near-saturated F36–W3 system. Treat the effect of interdomain exchange on surface enrichment as a question to be tested. Discuss salt crust formation as a later consequence unless its onset and feedback are independently quantified.
Abstract
Establish the problem: evaporation concentrates salt near the surface, while internal transport can redistribute it into the porous medium.
State the question: how does hydraulic contact between coarse and fine domains modify this redistribution and the resulting surface enrichment?
Summarize the approach: three-dimensional sodium MRI, water-content observations and mass measurements, combined with an experimentally constrained numerical model and targeted process comparisons.
Report the principal quantitative findings once established: domain-specific salt accumulation, the direction and magnitude of interfacial exchange, and the change caused by connecting the two domains.
Close with the physical implication and scope: when neighboring domains must be considered together to describe salt accumulation under sustained evaporative supply.
1 Introduction
Paragraph 1  Evaporation and salt accumulation
Explain how shallow saline-water supply sustains evaporation and transports dissolved salt toward the surface. Introduce the importance of internal redistribution for near-surface salinity and subsequent precipitation. Define the relevant setting as sustained supply rather than unrestricted drying of an isolated sample.
Paragraph 2  Competing transport processes
Explain the competition between upward advection, diffusion and density-driven downward convection. Summarize the different behavior previously observed in homogeneous F36 and W3 samples. Establish what that work already explains about permeability-dependent redistribution.
Paragraph 3  The role of a textural interface
Review how hydraulic contact and concentration gradients can produce lateral water and solute transport. Identify the unresolved issue for the present system: whether a convecting coarse domain modifies salt storage and surface enrichment in an adjacent fine domain. Distinguish this question from the established observation that heterogeneous media can support lateral redistribution.
Paragraph 4  Objectives of this study
Introduce the paired experimental and numerical investigation. State three objectives: characterize the evolving salt distribution in each domain; identify interfacial transport and its drivers; and quantify how connection changes surface enrichment relative to comparable isolated domains. End by explaining how MRI and numerical process comparisons address these objectives together.

4 Conclusions
