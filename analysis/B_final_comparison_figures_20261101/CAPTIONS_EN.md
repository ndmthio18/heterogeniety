# Figure captions and alt text

## Fig05_B_final_response_robustness

**Surface-storage connection effects after completion of the verification campaign.** (a) Connected-minus-blocked W3 salt inventory in the upper 3 mm with salinity buoyancy retained. The blue band spans eight tested numerical and initial-perturbation scenarios; the baseline and two prescribed hydraulic-conductivity corners are shown separately. (b) Individual 24 h effects for all ten B1 scenario pairs. (c) The interaction Δint=Δconn(B1)−Δconn(B0) for the baseline, finer grid and halved maximum time step. (d) Corresponding 24 h connection and interaction contrasts. Positive connection effects persist over the tested scenarios, while the buoyancy interaction changes sign during the simulation. Finite ranges are not confidence intervals or bounds on untested parameters. C1/C0 denote connected/blocked interface; B1/B0 denote retained/suppressed salinity-induced buoyancy, with gravity and reference hydrostatic forcing retained. Salt masses use 58.443 g/mol. All simulations reach 24 h of evaporation at RH=0.

Alt text: All ten tested B1 pairs yield a positive upper-3-mm W3 connection effect, around 4 mg at 24 hours. Numerical and perturbation results cluster closely; two conductivity corners give about 3.98 and 4.76 mg. The buoyancy interaction is initially weakly positive, peaks around 12 hours, then becomes approximately minus 4 mg at 24 hours, consistently across baseline, fine grid and smaller time step.

## Fig04_B_final_supply_and_partitioning

**Persistence of supply rerouting and depth-dependent salt partitioning.** (a) Cumulative W3 bottom salt input for connected and blocked B1 cases. (b) Connected-case interfacial advection, diffusion and their sum, positive from F36 to W3. (c) Whole-W3 connected-minus-blocked budget contributions, with the upper-3-mm response shown separately as a subset of the whole-side response. (d) Depth-band connection effects at 24 h. Points in d indicate the baseline; whiskers span either the eight numerical/perturbation scenarios or the baseline plus the two conductivity corners. The ranges are finite-scenario extrema, not statistical intervals. Category-connecting lines in a–c are visual guides and do not represent a continuous parameter trajectory. Large interfacial supply is accompanied by reduced bottom input; most of the additional W3 storage is below the upper 3 mm.

Alt text: Across all ten B1 scenarios, connected W3 receives about 22 mg from its bottom compared with approximately 237 mg when blocked. Lateral advection supplies roughly 292 mg while diffusion opposes it by roughly 35 mg. Bottom-supply and interface changes leave about 42 mg of additional whole-side storage, mostly at 3–20 mm depth, with roughly 4 mg in the upper 3 mm.

## FigS_B_final_numerical_and_plume_limits

**Metric-specific numerical limitations and sensitivity of plume diagnostics.** (a) Selected 24 h relative differences divided by the registered working tolerances; values above one are failures and are marked with open symbols. The reference denominator is the fine-grid or smaller-time-step value. Tolerances are 2% for surface-storage increments and 5% for the plotted transport and descending-volume metrics. The complete check table is supplied separately. (b) Non-monotone grid dependence of the connected F36 core descending-volume fraction. (c) Descending-volume fraction and (d) persistent core-downflow onset for baseline, phase and amplitude variants. Core descent uses downward Darcy speed greater than 1e-8 m/s, and onset requires three consecutive ten-minute output frames. The core excludes depths outside 5–35 mm, points within 3 mm of the interface, and radii above 20 mm. This operational onset is not a linear-instability threshold or an MRI detection time. Stable W3 integral effects do not establish convergence of plume fields.

Alt text: Several integral metrics pass, but F36 surface increments, connected core descending-volume fraction and selected W3 vertical net fluxes fail the fine-grid working tolerances. The connected core fraction changes non-monotonically from about 0.083 to 0.169 to 0.114. Blocked-case plume geometry and onset vary with perturbation phase and amplitude despite stable W3 connection effects.

## FigS_B_final_local_transport_compensation

**Local surface budgets across the completed scenario set.** Connected-minus-blocked cumulative transport contributions at 24 h for the W3 upper (a) 2 mm and (b) 3 mm, with buoyancy retained. (c) Contributions to the upper-3-mm buoyancy interaction for the baseline, fine grid and halved maximum time step. (d) Evolution of upper-3-mm lateral net, vertical net and storage differences on the baseline and fine grids. Upper-2-mm lateral and vertical advective/diffusive fluxes are independently recorded and checked against storage. For the upper 3 mm, lateral net exchange, denoted by an asterisk, is inferred from storage change minus vertical input; it is not an independent closure test, and no lateral advective/diffusive split is available. Vertical fluxes are integrated over accepted time steps and weighted consistently with fractional-cell storage. Large opposing contributions yield a small positive storage difference in all tested B1 pairs. This discrete-budget interpretation does not imply that every transport metric meets the numerical convergence tolerance.

Alt text: Across grid, time-step, perturbation and conductivity variants, increased lateral supply and reduced vertical input largely cancel. The finer-grid upper-3-mm connected-minus-blocked contributions are approximately minus 103.09 mg from vertical advection, plus 8.61 mg from vertical diffusion and plus 98.78 mg of inferred lateral net input, leaving about 4.31 mg. The buoyancy interaction also consists of opposing changes in several transport terms.
